# Causes of Dependence Between Extreme Floods

This repository contains the code supporting the analyses presented in:

**Deidda, C., Rahimi, L., & De Michele, C. (2021).**
*Causes of dependence between extreme floods.*
**Environmental Research Letters, 16(8), 084002.**
https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/ac07d5

## Overview

Understanding the dependence between extreme floods occurring at different locations is important for the assessment of spatial and compound flood risk.

Spatial dependence between extreme river discharges is often associated with the simultaneous occurrence of flood events. However, extreme floods may exhibit statistical dependence even when their occurrence is not strongly synchronized.

In this study, we investigate the origins of spatial dependence between annual maximum river discharges observed at river stations across the United Kingdom.

We show that the dependence between extreme floods can result from a combination of:

* **co-occurrence of annual maxima**;
* **similarities in climatological characteristics**; and
* **similarities in hydrological catchment response**.

The results therefore suggest that spatial dependence between extreme floods cannot be explained by synchronization alone.

## Methodology

Pairwise dependence between annual maximum instantaneous river discharges is evaluated using **Kendall's τ**.

To investigate whether dependence is primarily generated by the simultaneous occurrence of annual maxima, we compare the original dependence with a conditional measure calculated using non-co-occurring flood events.

The analysis is complemented by measures describing differences between catchments in terms of their climatological and hydrological characteristics.

Three dissimilarity indices are used to investigate the relationship between catchment similarity and flood dependence.

The resulting **dependence–dissimilarity relationships** provide a framework for exploring the physical origins of statistical dependence between extreme floods.

## Case study

The analysis is applied to river stations located across the **United Kingdom**.

The large number of catchments with different climatic and hydrological characteristics allows us to investigate how flood dependence changes with geographical separation, flood co-occurrence, and catchment similarity.

The results show that statistically dependent annual maxima can occur even for catchments located far apart and characterized by little or no simultaneous occurrence of their annual maximum floods.

## Main finding

The study proposes a broader interpretation of the origins of spatial dependence between extreme floods:

> **Flood dependence can emerge from the combined effects of event co-occurrence and similarities in climatological and hydrological catchment characteristics.**

This highlights that statistical dependence between spatial extremes does not necessarily imply that the corresponding extreme events occur simultaneously.

## Data

The analysis uses hydrological and catchment information for river stations in the United Kingdom.

Raw observational data are not distributed through this repository. Users wishing to reproduce the complete analysis should obtain the corresponding datasets from their original data providers and adapt the input paths used in the scripts.

## Requirements

The analyses are implemented primarily in **R**.

Required packages depend on the individual analysis scripts. Users should check the `library()` calls at the beginning of each script for the corresponding dependencies.
